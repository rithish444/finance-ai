# Fixed Deposits (FD) & Recurring Deposits (RD)

## What is a Fixed Deposit?
A Fixed Deposit (FD) is a financial instrument provided by Indian scheduled commercial banks and NBFCs where an investor deposits a lump sum for a predetermined tenor at a fixed rate of interest. A Recurring Deposit (RD) operates similarly on a monthly installment basis.

## Key Attributes:
1. **Capital Safety**: Bank deposits are insured by the DICGC (Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation, an RBI subsidiary) up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank (principal + interest).
2. **Predictable Returns**: The interest rate is locked for the entire tenure, insulating the investor from market fluctuations.
3. **Liquidity**: Premature withdrawal is usually permissible, subject to a nominal penalty (typically 0.5% - 1.0% on interest).

## When to Use FDs:
- Goals maturing within 1 to 3 years.
- Conservative portion of an emergency fund.
- Parking lump sums intended for near-term major purchases (e.g. house downpayment within 18 months).

## Limitations:
- Returns are fully taxable at the individual's income tax slab rate.
- After taxes and inflation (6%), real net returns are modest (often 0.5% - 1.5%).
